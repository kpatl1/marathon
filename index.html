Standing rule: upper body on rest days ✅

Yes, add it. Upper-body lifting has low systemic cost for a runner — it doesn't load the legs, doesn't deplete glycogen much, and doesn't meaningfully impair next-day running. The "keep rest days rested" principle exists to protect your legs and your recovery from running stress. Push/pull work is largely outside that.

Four rules to keep it from backfiring:

  • Cap it at ~40 min. The failure mode isn't lifting, it's a 90-minute session that eats the sleep you already don't have enough of.
  • Skip it on red Oura days. If readiness is under 55, a rest day means rest — that's systemic, not muscular.
  • Never the day before a long run or quality session if it involves heavy pressing overhead. Fatigued shoulders change your arm carriage and running posture late in a long run.
  • Taper it down from Oct 12 and stop entirely after Oct 24. Race week you want to feel light, not worked.

Chafing / groin rash — kit and protocol 🩹

Buy: Zeasorb-AF powder (absorbent + antifungal, covers both likely causes) · moisture-wicking boxer briefs, snug not loose — cotton is what causes this · Body Glide for prevention before runs, once healed.

Add if needed: bumps on hair follicles with white tips → benzoyl peroxide 4–5% wash (PanOxyl), once daily, leave 60 s. Not clearing in 5–7 days or a raised scaly edge spreading outward → clotrimazole 1% twice daily for 2 weeks, continuing a week past when it looks clear.

Protocol: shower after sweating → pat dry → air dry 5–10 min before dressing (this matters more than any product) → powder during the day, no creams or ointments while active (they trap moisture) → loose cotton or nothing at night → don't scratch, don't shave the area until healed.

Cautions: hydrocortisone for a few days max — it worsens fungal infections and thins groin skin. Barrier balms are for prevention on intact skin, not for active heat rash. See a doctor if it becomes painful, warm, firm, produces pus, or hasn't largely cleared in a week.

Prevention on long runs (this is the one that matters from Week 8): barrier balm before every run 10 mi+, wicking briefs, change out of damp clothes immediately. A 3-hour long run at mile 16 is where chafing actually ruins a day — and race day is 26.2.

⚠️ Staying healthy through the September ramp — the actual protocol

You've had two tendon flags at ~13 mi/week. The plan now climbs to ~30. Your heart and lungs will handle that easily; tendons and bone are the constraint, and they adapt on a much slower clock than fitness does. Ramp softened to: 17 → 20 → 25 → 30 → 24 (cutback) → 32 peak. Steps of roughly 20%, with a real down week before the peak.

Non-negotiables

  • Strength 2×/week, every week, no exceptions. This is the single highest-value thing on the calendar and it's the one that keeps getting cut. Both flags appeared during a month with zero leg work. If a week only has room for one session, make it legs, not a run.
  • Calf + posterior tib work every session: straight-leg and bent-knee heel raises, plus heel raises with the foot turned slightly out. Slow 3-second lower. This is what makes tendon tolerate volume.
  • 80% of running genuinely easy. You're doing this well — keep it. One quality session a week is plenty at this volume.
  • Never two hard days back to back, and never a long run within 24 hrs of a hard lift.
  • Soft surfaces where available — Rock Creek dirt, Lakefront, the Mall gravel — especially for long runs.

Rules that override the plan

  • The warmup test: if something is tight at the start, it should be looser by mile 2. If it's sharper, end the run there. No exceptions, no negotiating with yourself mid-run.
  • Two consecutive runs with the same pain → take 3 days off and cross-train. Three days costs nothing; three weeks costs the race.
  • Pinpoint tenderness on bone — you can find it with one fingertip — means stop running and see someone. That's the one injury that ends this.
  • Never add mileage and intensity in the same week. If a week goes up in volume, keep the quality session the same size as last week's.
  • Shoes: replace around 400 mi. Have your race pair broken in by mid-September and don't run the marathon in anything you haven't done a 10+ mile run in.

Missing a week to be cautious costs you almost nothing — you proved that Aug 14–17. Missing six weeks to a stress fracture ends it. When in doubt, take the smaller option.

Injury early-warning (act on these, don't push through)

Sharp shin pain that persists into walking, achilles stiffness lasting past the first mile, knee pain that worsens during a run → cut the run, take 2 days off, cross-train (bike/swim). Coming off a low run base into 13 weeks of building, calves/shins are the risk zone — that's why calf raises are in every leg day and mileage builds gradually with cutback weeks.

Tightness vs. bone pain — learn the difference, it decides everything: tightness or a diffuse ache that loosens as you warm up is muscle and tendon. Annoying, trainable around, responds to eccentric calf work and ankle mobility. Pain you can pinpoint with one fingertip on the shin bone, or that sharpens the longer you run, is bone stress — that one means stop running and get it looked at. The warmup is your daily test.

Active flag — right posterior tibial tendon (Aug 25): inner ankle, just behind and below the medial malleolus, tight and painful through ankle rotation. This tendon supports the arch and controls pronation, so it takes the most load at push-off and on uneven ground — and you have two twisted right ankles in your history. It responds well to early backing-off and badly to being run through. Rules: tightness that loosens as you warm up = run, but keep it easy. Tenderness you can pinpoint with a fingertip, swelling, or pain rising onto that foot's toes = stop and get it looked at. Go easy on eccentric heel drops and calf raises on that side until it's quiet, and add ankle circles + single-leg balance daily. Cross-training substitutes cleanly here — bike and pool both keep the aerobic base without loading the tendon.

Your Strava history says: two twisted ankles (May) → do the band walks + add 30 s/leg single-leg balance daily, it's free. Recurring blisters (Sept, April) → hydrocolloid bandages preemptively on known hot spots before every run 10 mi+, and lock in your sock/shoe combo by Week 8 — race day feet are non-negotiable. Also: the Brooks with support felt "WAY better" per your own April notes — make the shoe decision early and buy a second pair by mid-September so it's broken in for Nov 1.
